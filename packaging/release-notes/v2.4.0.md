This release is about your privacy. What you write about your attacks is now encrypted in the database, the app asks for your explicit consent before keeping your health data, you can delete your account from inside the apps, and the privacy policy now says exactly what happens to your data. Nothing changes in how you log an attack.

## New

- **Your notes, triggers and medications are encrypted in the database.** Each value is encrypted on its own, so a copy of the database shows none of what you wrote: the medication, triggers and notes of every attack, the names and schedule notes of your medications, and feedback answers. Everything you logged before this release has been encrypted too. By @crmne
- **I ask for your consent before keeping your health data.** Signing up now takes ticking a box that says, in plain words and in English, German, Italian and Spanish, what the app does with your health data and how to withdraw your consent. If you signed up before, you'll be asked once, the next time you open the app. You can delete your account instead, and export your logs first on the website. By @crmne
- **Delete your account from Settings, in the apps too.** It asks for your password again, so nobody holding your unlocked phone can wipe your history, and deletes your logs, photos, medications, share links and feedback. If you can't sign in, [this page](https://clusterheadachetracker.com/delete-account) explains what to do. By @crmne
- **A privacy policy that says exactly what happens to your data.** It now covers the iOS and Android apps, what stays on your phone, page counts, error reports, how long data is kept, how to delete it, and whom to complain to. By @crmne

## Fixed

- **Pages no longer hand your IP address to Google.** The DM Sans font now comes from our own server instead of Google Fonts. By @crmne
- **Error reports stay in Germany.** They now go to my own error tracker on our server in Germany instead of Honeybadger, and name your account by its number only. By @crmne
- **Widgets forget your attack status when you're signed out.** After a session expired, a sign-out on another device or a deleted account, the last account's status used to stay on your home screen. Needs the next version of the iOS or Android app. By @crmne
- **App Store and Google Play builds update through their store.** They no longer point at GitHub downloads or show the GitHub Sponsors links, which the stores don't allow. By @crmne
- **The attack timer stays still with Reduce Motion on.** Its digits no longer roll every second. By @crmne

## Known limitations

- Attack times, intensities, doses, ratings and barometric pressure readings aren't encrypted this way, so the app can sort and chart them.
- The encryption keys are kept on the same server, so encryption protects a copy of the database, not a server someone has taken over. Photos were already encrypted on disk, and backups are encrypted as a whole.
